# Evidence Protocol

## Evidence hierarchy

Prefer evidence in this order when relevant:

1. First-party behavioral, transaction, CRM, experiment, interview, survey, or win/loss data
2. Official statistics, regulations, filings, and company disclosures
3. Transparent peer-reviewed or reputable third-party research
4. Competitor materials, product reviews, communities, and job postings
5. Search, social, and media signals

Lower-ranked sources can reveal signals but should not be treated as proof of customer demand or willingness to pay.

## Claim states

| State | Meaning | Allowed language |
|---|---|---|
| Verified evidence | Directly supported by suitable primary or authoritative evidence | “The data shows…” |
| Corroborated signal | Multiple independent sources point in the same direction | “Several sources indicate…” |
| Reasonable inference | Interpretation logically derived from cited evidence | “This suggests…” |
| Unvalidated hypothesis | Plausible claim awaiting suitable evidence | “A hypothesis to test is…” |
| Evidence gap | Available evidence cannot support a conclusion | “We cannot yet determine…” |

## Source record

For each material source capture title, publisher or owner, URL or supplied dataset name, publication or observation date, access date, geography, segment, method when known, and limitations.

## Opportunity register

Use these columns:

| Opportunity | Supporting evidence | Status | Confidence | Missing evidence | Validation work | Decision unlocked |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Confidence must reflect evidence quality and agreement, not the analyst's enthusiasm.

## Missing evidence response

When evidence is insufficient:

1. State the exact conclusion that cannot be made.
2. Preserve useful observations as signals or hypotheses.
3. Identify the cheapest reliable method to reduce uncertainty.
4. Define what result would support, weaken, or reject the hypothesis.
5. Explain which business decision the research enables.

Possible methods include customer interviews, surveys, message or landing-page tests, willingness-to-pay studies, CRM analysis, funnel or cohort analysis, win/loss review, search-demand analysis, and competitor or channel tests. Select a method based on the uncertainty, not convenience.
