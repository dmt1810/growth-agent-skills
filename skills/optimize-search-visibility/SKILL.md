# Optimize Search Visibility

## Workflow

1. Define the business outcomes, audiences, markets, search surfaces, conversion actions, and measurement access.
2. Establish a baseline for crawlability, indexation, rankings or visibility, traffic quality, conversions, and citations when data exists.
3. Audit technical access, rendering, canonicalization, site structure, templates, performance, structured data, and duplication.
4. Research queries, entities, questions, jobs, and intent; group them into topics and journeys.
5. Map demand to existing and proposed pages. Identify gaps, cannibalization, weak differentiation, and unsupported claims.
6. Improve information architecture, on-page relevance, answer clarity, internal linking, evidence, and machine-readable context.
7. Develop authority through original evidence, useful tools, expert contributions, references, and legitimate distribution.
8. Prioritize fixes by impact, confidence, effort, dependency, and time to learn.
9. Define monitoring, test plans, and decision thresholds.

Read [references/search-audit.md](references/search-audit.md) for the audit and prioritization schema.

## Guardrails

- Do not guarantee rankings, traffic, citations, or revenue.
- Cite current official documentation for platform-specific claims when browsing is available.
- Distinguish directly observed issues from likely issues requiring analytics, crawling, or search-console access.
- Do not recommend manipulative links, hidden content, keyword stuffing, or scaled low-value pages.
- Treat AI visibility as an evolving distribution surface; focus on accessible, clear, well-supported information rather than undocumented tricks.

## Required Output

1. Executive findings
2. Baseline and data limitations
3. Technical findings
4. Demand, intent, and entity map
5. Content and architecture findings
6. Authority and citation findings
7. Prioritized roadmap
8. Page-level recommendations where requested
9. Measurement plan and next crawl or data needs
